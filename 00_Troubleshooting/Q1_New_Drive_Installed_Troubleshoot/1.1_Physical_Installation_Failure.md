# Project 2 — Enterprise Drive Validation
## Troubleshooting Question 1

### Q1.1 — Physical Seating / Installation Failure

### Problem

A new enterprise drive is installed in an enterprise server, but the system does not detect the drive.

The drive may be:

- NVMe SSD
- SAS SSD
- SATA SSD
- SAS HDD
- SATA HDD

This troubleshooting case focuses specifically on **improper physical seating / installation** as the possible root cause.

---

## Expected Flow

```text
Drive physically installed
        ↓
Correct bay / slot
        ↓
Drive properly seated
        ↓
Connector engaged
        ↓
Backplane / interface path established
        ↓
Platform detects drive
        ↓
OS/interface detects drive
        ↓
Drive becomes usable
```

---

## Troubleshooting Approach

### 1. Establish Baseline

Record:

- Server
- Bay/slot
- Drive model
- Drive serial / asset identifier
- Drive type
- Firmware, where applicable
- Time of installation
- Existing drive inventory

### 2. Verify Physical Installation

Check:

- Correct bay/slot
- Correct drive
- Drive fully inserted
- Carrier properly engaged
- Retention/latch secured
- Correct orientation
- No obstruction
- No obvious connector damage

### 3. Identify the Physical Condition

Example observation:

```text
Drive is not fully seated.
Carrier latch is not completely engaged.
```

At this stage:

```text
Observation → Drive not fully seated
Hypothesis  → Improper seating caused non-detection
```

The hypothesis is not yet the confirmed RCA.

### 4. Correct the Physical Condition

Following the platform's approved procedure:

```text
Reseat drive
   ↓
Secure carrier
   ↓
Return platform to required state
```

### 5. Verify Detection Recovery

Compare before and after:

```text
Before:
Drive not detected

After proper seating:
Drive detected
```

Verify the downstream path as applicable to the drive type.

For NVMe:

```text
Physical
   ↓
Platform/BMC
   ↓
BIOS
   ↓
PCIe
   ↓
NVMe
   ↓
Namespace
   ↓
Block device
```

For SAS/SATA:

```text
Physical
   ↓
Platform/BMC
   ↓
BIOS/controller
   ↓
SAS/SATA path
   ↓
OS driver
   ↓
Block device
```

### 6. Isolate Drive vs Bay

Use controlled comparative testing where applicable:

```text
Affected drive + original bay
Known-good drive + original bay
Affected drive + known-good compatible bay
```

The exact test depends on the server architecture and approved procedure.

### 7. Establish RCA

Example evidence chain:

```text
Drive not detected
        ↓
Physical inspection found improper seating
        ↓
Drive correctly reseated
        ↓
Platform detected drive
        ↓
OS detected drive
        ↓
Known-good drive also works in the bay
```

### Root Cause

> Improper physical seating of the newly installed drive prevented the expected hardware connection from being established, resulting in drive non-detection.

### Supporting Evidence

1. Drive was not fully seated.
2. Drive was absent before correction.
3. Correct seating restored detection.
4. Comparative testing supported that the bay/path itself was functional.

---

# Interview Presentation

### Interview Question

**How would you troubleshoot a newly installed enterprise drive that is not detected?**

### Answer

> I would start with the physical installation because the software stack cannot detect a drive if the physical path is not properly established. I would verify the correct bay or slot, drive seating, carrier engagement, connector condition, and the platform installation procedure. If I find that the drive is not fully seated, I would correct the physical condition and then verify whether detection recovers at the platform and OS levels. I would also use controlled comparison with a known-good drive or compatible bay where appropriate to isolate the drive from the bay or backplane. I would consider physical seating the root cause only when the evidence shows the drive was not detected before correction and detection consistently recovered after the physical issue was corrected.
